Field note 2 · covers October 2026

What the codes find in the trial registry

With the codes harvested and reviewed (previous note), I searched ClinicalTrials.gov, through AACT, with the INN and with the accepted codes, using one matching rule for both. A trial counts as found only via a code when the INN is not in its registered intervention name.

29%of drug–trial pairs for the 2025 FDA novel approvals are found only via a code (197 of 679)
48%of their Phase 1 pairs are found only via a code (100 of 210)
5.7%for the top-selling medicines of 12 large companies (631 of 11,035)

One substance, three names

Zoliflodacin (Nuzolvence, approved 2025) was AZD0914 at AstraZeneca and ETX0914 at Entasis Therapeutics. NIAID's 2018 news release says it in one line: "formerly known as ETX0914 and AZD0914". The trials do not say it in the fields a search uses.

Per drug

For 165 of the 197 drug–trial pairs found only via a code in the FDA collection, the INN is in neither trial title either. 26 of the 30 products have such trials.

Where in a drug's history

The early and the old history of a drug is under its codes. For recent approvals that is almost half of Phase 1. For big sellers with large INN-registered programmes the share is small overall, but it is a fifth to a quarter of their trials started before 2015.

Why it matters

Anyone who searches trials by drug name, for a systematic review, a safety question, real-world evidence, or a competitor overview, misses the early and the older trials unless the codes are in the search. Most for recently approved drugs, where the INN came late.
